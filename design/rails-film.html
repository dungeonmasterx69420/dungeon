Three film treatments

The rails were plain vertical hairlines at a 92px pitch. Each of these keeps that pitch and that weight and draws it out of the thing the site is for. Same mask, same z-index, no accent colour — a page swaps one for another with a single class and nothing else moves.

The strip

.rails

35mm stock standing on end. Perforations ride the rails at the existing pitch and a frame line crosses every fourth one, which is how film is actually pulled. It is the smallest possible step from what is there now — the rhythm of the page does not change, only what the rhythm is made of.

The aperture

.rails .rails--aperture

The same 92px spacing bent round a lens: rings out from the iris, six blades closing on it, and the blades masked to the iris itself — a spoke that runs to the edge of the page is a starburst, not a lens. The centre is a variable, so the lens can sit behind the key card on a wide screen and stay centred on a phone.

The gate

.rails .rails--gate

What the camera shows you: thirds, crop marks at the corners, a focus bracket in the middle, and the frame counter ticking along the top edge at the house pitch. This one is inset to the content column and closed at the bottom rather than faded, because a frame that runs off the screen is not a frame. It reads as a composition rather than a texture, which suits a hero and would be too loud on a form.

What it was

plain hairlines, 92px

Kept here to compare against. Nothing wrong with it — it just says printing plate rather than film.

Check every one of these at 390px and at 1440px. The two things a static read will not catch are horizontal overflow and console errors, and a background that is positioned off the centre of the viewport is exactly where overflow comes from.
